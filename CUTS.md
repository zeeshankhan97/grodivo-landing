# What we cut, and why

The founder's stated test is whether an agency can rationalise his input and translate it.
So here is the editing, not just the output.

---

## Cut because he killed it

Every banned item is absent — not softened, absent. The ones that were hardest to give up:

**Six-persona role selector.** The current site lets a visitor self-identify. It is the
single biggest driver of "10x more screens". One audience frame only: M&A. The three
permitted buyers appear once, as a label strip, costing zero body copy.

**Employee surveys, engagement, sentiment.** This is the vocabulary that makes the product
read as HR tooling to a PE buyer. Removing it forced the numbers to carry the argument
instead — which is what a dealmaker responds to anyway.

**TAP Self-Aware™, Culture Predict™, Culture Publish™, individual career growth.** Gone.
Only three modules survive, and each gets one line.

---

## Cut because structure should carry it

**The section headline in Section 2.** We wrote one, then deleted it. "$100M of business
case revenue at risk" is a better opening than any label we could put above it. Beat 1 *is*
the headline.

**Numbered markers (01–05) on the narrative spine.** The order is mandated, so numbering
was defensible — but the hero already uses 01–08 for the dimensions. Two numeric systems on
one page would read as decoration. Semantic labels do the same work without the collision.

**A second CTA in the masthead.** With two sections there is nothing to navigate to. The
masthead is now a masthead: who made it, what it's called.

**Icons on the module cards.** Three icons would have said less than three names. The only
icons on the page are two arrows.

---

## Cut because it was decoration pretending to be information

**Photography.** None. For a PE audience that expects handshake stock imagery, a page that
looks like an oscilloscope is the risk we chose to take — it is also the only honest way to
say "AI-native" without using the word.

**Ambient motion.** The calibration sweep fires once and never loops. A looping animation on
a measurement panel undercuts the claim that the number is real.

**A zero baseline on the instrument.** No score falls below 40 and there is no y-axis, so a
zero baseline bought nothing but empty panel. Every delta still scales identically, which is
the only comparison the chart makes.

---

## Cut during build, for a reason worth stating

**The 8-across periodic table.** Built it first — it was symmetrical and it looked like the
existing brand artifact. But at that width "DIFFERENTIATION" either broke mid-word or
dropped to 9px. We measured it rather than argued about it: only 9.4px fits on one line.
So groups became columns, which is what a real periodic table does anyway, and every
dimension now carries its full name at a readable size. Productivity holding only two
elements is left visible rather than padded — the framework is 3/3/2 and the table should
say so.

**The oversized display type.** The first pass set the headline at 55px and pushed the CTA
below the fold. The ceiling is now set by measurement, not taste.

---

## Kept, deliberately, against the instinct to redesign

**Inter.** It is the incumbent brand face. Replacing it would have been a change with no
argument behind it. It stays as the body face; the personality is spent on the display and
mono roles instead.

**"Map Your Gap™" as the primary CTA.** It is already an imperative and it is already
theirs. A new CTA would have been us talking instead of them.

---

## The numbers

- Hero body copy: **20 words** (cap: 25)
- Section 2 body copy: **88 words** (cap: 120) — every visible word, including
  module names and ledger labels. Prose alone is 73.
- Sections: **2**
- Modules named: **3**
- Audiences named: **3**
- Meanings assigned to red: **1** — a gap at or above the critical threshold.
  Wherever red appears, that is what it says.

The word budget came in under cap in both sections. We did not spend the remainder.
